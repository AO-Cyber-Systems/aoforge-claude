# TRD 44-01: Uncap executor/verifier and make truncation a resumable INCOMPLETE outcome

<objective>
The 50/30 `maxTurns` caps are DevFlow's own setting. They truncate 38% of executor runs and 37%
of verifier runs (44-EVIDENCE §1, DF-01). The orchestrator then reads a truncation as a failure:
it respawns fresh once and then skips every dependent. This TRD:

- removes the caps (guard-no-progress.js is the runaway guard);
- makes the executor leave a resumable trail: a commit per task and a `## Progress` checkpoint;
- teaches execute-objective a third outcome, INCOMPLETE, resumed via SendMessage.

It also carries three small edits to files it owns:
- yolo counts as autonomous between waves (AUT-06, DF-14);
- no `sleep`-poll in the executor (AUT-07, CC-02);
- the legacy `~/.claude/agents/planner.md` read line in execute-objective is removed (AUT-03, DF-04).

Purpose: executors run to completion, and a cut-short run resumes with its context instead of being
redone or wrongly failed.
</objective>

<anti_patterns>
- Raising the cap to some other number. The requirement is removal; `guard-no-progress.js`
  detects repetition, which is the real runaway signal.
- Treating INCOMPLETE as a failure variant (retry counter shared with failures, dependents
  skipped). It is its own outcome with its own budget.
- Resuming with a fresh `Task(...)` spawn. The resume MUST be `SendMessage` to the same task id,
  because that is what keeps the context. A fresh respawn is the fallback after 3 resumes.
- Asking the user "Ready for wave N?" / "Continue?" between waves in yolo mode. This produced
  58 of the 186 human nudges.
- Changing the non-autonomous FAILURE prompt. Yolo only changes between-wave continuation; a
  real failure in yolo still follows the non-autonomous failure handling.
- Using `sleep 30 && check` loops anywhere in the executor instructions.
</anti_patterns>
